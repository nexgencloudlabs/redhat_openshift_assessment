Red Hat OpenShift Assessment Tool

Assess your OpenShift platform against best practices for availability, resilience, disaster recovery, performance, scalability, security, compliance, and operational excellence

This assessment framework evaluates Red Hat OpenShift environments against six foundational pillars to identify architectural gaps, operational risks, control weaknesses, and optimization opportunities across container and virtualization workloads.

Framework Origin

Proprietary assessment model informed by enterprise delivery experience

Please note: While this assessment evaluates Red Hat technologies and aligns with officially documented best practices, this Well-Architected Framework is not an official methodology published by Red Hat. Rather, it is a proprietary assessment model forged from our consulting team's extensive field experience, lessons learned, and successful enterprise deployments across multiple complex customer engagements.

Advisory Baseline
Assessment Methodology

Assessment methodology and review approach

The primary objective of this assessment is to identify architectural gaps, highlight areas of operational risk, and produce a practical remediation roadmap. It provides a structured way to assess OpenShift design choices, operational controls, and enterprise readiness across all six pillars.

6 Pillars

Assessment focus

  • Evaluate the architectural fitness of the existing OpenShift environment.
  • Surface risks that affect uptime, recoverability, security, performance, and operating model maturity.
  • Provide traceable insights that support prioritization, remediation planning, and executive reporting.
  • Support cloud-native and virtualization-enabled operating models within highly regulated environments.

Why this framework matters

A mature OpenShift platform must do more than host applications. It must withstand node and site failures, preserve recoverability, enforce security by design, operate through automated controls, and optimize cost and capacity over time. This landing page organizes the framework into a clear structure that can later be extended into a full interactive assessment tool.

Architecture-first review

Each pillar measures whether critical platform design decisions have been implemented in a way that supports stable, enterprise-grade operations.

Operational evidence

Controls are intended to be validated through observable configuration, runtime behavior, policy enforcement, and supporting operational artefacts.

Outcome-driven remediation

The assessment is designed to move beyond findings alone by enabling a prioritized roadmap tied to resilience, risk, efficiency, and business continuity outcomes.

Business Outcomes

Target state for enterprise OpenShift operations

By assessing the platform against this model, the organization moves beyond infrastructure provisioning toward a more disciplined cloud operating model. The resulting insights support stronger architectural decisions, clearer risk visibility, and more efficient platform operations.

Outcome Areas

Reduce single points of failure

Strengthen platform continuity across control plane, worker, network, storage, and ingress layers.

Improve recoverability

Validate backup, restoration, and multi-cluster orchestration capabilities for critical business workloads.

Harden security posture

Reinforce access, workload isolation, encryption, image integrity, and continuous compliance enforcement.

Optimize performance, cost and capacity

Right-size resources, improve autoscaling coverage, reduce sprawl, and increase platform efficiency across the OpenShift estate.
